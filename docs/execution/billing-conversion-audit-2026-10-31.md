# Billing conversion audit — 31 October 2026

## Scope and evidence

Goal: help a Free workspace choose and buy the right offer with less searching and less doubt, while keeping subscriber management and payment recovery clear. Entry points include `/billing`, a Pricing deep link, and an in-app upgrade prompt. A successful purchase is a provider-confirmed payment followed by an authoritative webhook grant; an opened checkout or closed overlay is not proof of either.

This audit used the current Billing source and tests, the public DeclutrMail Pricing page, a synthetic Free workspace rendered in a local Next browser, and read-only competitor research. No production billing API, catalog, checkout, subscription, refund, or webhook was changed or called. The browser smoke did not submit a purchase. Storybook's existing Vite `Unexpected identifier 'assert'` error prevented that renderer. The PR has no preview route; a separate, unpublished local review worktree serves a synthetic Billing screen at `http://localhost:3017/billing-local-review` for owner sign-off, with `/api` writes blocked.

External patterns observed on 31 October:

- [Clean Email plans](https://clean.email/plans) places a buy action on a priced plan and opens its secure checkout overlay directly. Its overlay still asks for contact and payment details. Its [subscription help](https://clean.email/help/accounts/free-trial-and-subscriptions) documents an in-app Upgrade entry point and a More Plans escape hatch.
- [Notion's plan-change help](https://www.notion.com/help/upgrade-or-downgrade-your-plan) describes Settings → Upgrade plan → select plan → order summary → Upgrade now. The summary is part of the decision, not an optional footnote.
- [1Password's billing help](https://support.1password.com/manage-subscription/) groups plan, status, renewal, payment method, and invoices in Billing. This is a useful pattern for existing subscribers, whose primary job differs from a Free visitor's.
- [Todoist's subscription help](https://www.todoist.com/help/account-and-billing/billing/set-up-a-todoist-pro-subscription-PLxdCDaH9) describes Settings → Subscription → Upgrade → billing choice → checkout.
- [Baymard's checkout research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields) argues that the number of fields and perceived effort matter more than raw step count. We therefore treat extra scrolling, hidden choices, and uncertainty as friction alongside clicks. This is general checkout research, not a measured DeclutrMail lift.

## Flow and state checks

Free path: current access → Pass/Plus choice → nearby product-specific review → existing Paddle overlay → pending notice → webhook-confirmed grant. Dismissing review returns to the same choice. Refresh, duplicate click, cross-tab pending work, checkout closure, and ambiguous payment outcomes retain the existing reservation and reconciliation behavior.

Management path: active subscription, complimentary access, Pass-only access, scheduled change, cancellation, past-due recovery, invoices, and billing-disabled notices keep their established controls and order. The picker is not raised above recovery for a paid subscriber. Invoices remain available after a subscription ends. The local change did not alter provider requests or entitlement calculations.

The synthetic browser smoke confirmed the Free desktop/tablet hierarchy, the Pass review's visible product, price, non-renewal, tax caveat, and CTA. It initially showed PH30, which the later customer report exposed as misleading. A subsequent phone-width local walkthrough confirmed the Free, active Plus, and past-due ordering and revealed that `scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest' })` left much of the selected review below the viewport; it now uses `block: 'start'`. A local checkout click returned the expected disabled response from the review harness. Automated tests cover Free/paid section order, Pass/Plus/legacy review placement, focus return, and Pass selection clearing under a payment lock. A real provider checkout remains unverified in this change; do not report a production conversion improvement from this audit.

Verification after the final UI revision: 5,257 web tests passed and 3 were skipped; workspace typecheck, changed-file ESLint, production web build, all 50 bundle budgets, and 32 static-route assertions passed. The independent reviewer found no provider/lock mutation and identified the focus-return and Pass-lock gaps, which were fixed and covered by focused tests. A final post-review focus change received a focused test and typecheck pass. Test fixtures emitted localhost connection warnings without changing the passing result.

After the local scroll adjustment, 226 focused Billing tests and the web typecheck passed. The full suite and build results above preceded this one-line adjustment; CI is rerunning on the updated PR.

## PH30 customer report and local correction — 31 October

A customer reported that a purchase succeeded but the published PH30 code was rejected. Read-only inspection of the exact public Pricing page showed the banner promising 30% off through Nov 3. The checked-in live discount ID is `FILL_ME`, while `checkoutDiscountId()` omits that placeholder from Paddle transactions. `promoBanner()` previously checked only the date. A regression test reproduced the mismatch: the live checkout helper returned `null` while the banner still returned PH30 text.

The local correction makes `promoBanner()` also require a provisioned live discount ID. The Pricing banner, Billing purchase review, and code-entry hint all use that helper, so the unsupported offer disappears together. This is a copy guard, not a live discount repair. The actual live Paddle catalog, this customer's receipt/charge, and any remedy were not inspected or changed. Do not claim PH30 is usable again without read-only live catalog verification and a sandbox plus controlled live rehearsal under the billing cutover rules. The production site continues to advertise PH30 until a separately approved release of this correction; this PR remains under the owner's no-merge hold.

## Decision and measurement

This is a presentation and interaction change. Keep the existing Paddle.js overlay, server-side adapter, webhook authority, refund policy, and no-double-charge locks. After release, first verify the exact deployed UI and monitor purchase errors and verified purchases. Compare eligible, consented Free visitors before and after with device and offer segmentation; do not claim causation from a small or mixed sample. If the overlay remains the dominant drop-off, use sandbox research on that surface before touching production billing configuration.

**Release hold:** The owner requested local verification before any merge. Keep this PR open and out of the merge queue until the owner reviews the local preview and explicitly lifts the no-merge instruction. A later release still needs required checks and reviews plus a sandbox checkout rehearsal; this local synthetic screen does not prove the provider payment path.
