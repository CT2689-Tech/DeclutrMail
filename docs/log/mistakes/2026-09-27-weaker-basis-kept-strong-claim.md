## 2026-09-27 — A protection kept on a weaker basis still claimed the stronger one, and lost it on the next lapse
**PR:** #786 (https://github.com/CT2689-Tech/DeclutrMail/pull/786)
**Caught by:** independent adversarial review of #786 at 10558881 (orchestrator-requested)
**What happened:** The narrowed release kept an importance protection for a sender whose Primary labels had only tied, so its tab read `unknown`. Two things still assumed the strong basis, a Primary majority. (1) The reason sentence said Gmail marked "this Primary-inbox sender" important, which the Senders page contradicted with "Gmail: no single tab". (2) The keep rule was an exception keyed on the stored reason being `gmail_important`. Once the reason was corrected to `starred`, the star's lapse released the sender, although the importance the exception protected still held. The same review found two gaps next to it. A protection change never expired the decision that names it, so a lapsed star read "Protected because you've starred…" for up to 7 days. And a sweep job that committed and then failed its 60 s budget could queue a second re-score on retry.
**Correct approach:** Treat the weaker basis as a rule of its own. When an existing protection's reason lapses, re-evaluate every automatic reason and correct it in place to importance if importance still holds on Primary-labelled mail; release only when nothing holds; new grants stay strict. Delete the claim the weaker basis cannot back, so the copy states only the count and the verb. Expire the decision of every sender whose protection changed. Key the re-score job id on the sweep tick, so a retry is a no-op.
**Rule:** When a state can now be kept on a weaker basis than the one that granted it, re-derive its copy and every transition out of it from that basis, not from the stored label.
**Enforcement update:** tests (each red against a negative control): the importance reason names no Primary inbox; a tie-kept sender whose star lapses stays protected as `gmail_important`; every released, corrected or granted protection expires its decision and a steady one does not; one job id per sweep tick.
